Messenger feature panels

Today Messenger opens a wing of the Full Chat layout as a tab or dialog, so lore, cast, presets and trackers look like a different app. Here every one of those wings is a native Messenger surface in Discord's architecture: panels dock on the right of the chat, switch from the header, and share one frame. Bunzai keeps its tokens, fonts and the button family. Only structure is borrowed. Page width 1440. Headers, rail and sidebar are sketched only as far as the panels need them.

0. The four surface kinds

Side 400 px panel docked right of the chat, like members or threads Wide 600 px inspector, same slot, like a thread opened wide Popout floating card on avatar click or right click Layer large floating window over the dimmed app with left nav and X, like Discord settings (section 6)

Decision. One panel at a time, in one slot. Every program from programs.ts becomes one of these four kinds, drawn by one shared frame (title, scope line, pop-out, close). That replaces today's mix of tab, dialog and floating pop-out (messenger-chrome.tsx program tab or overlay). Small lists and forms are Side. Anything with a master/detail editor is Wide. Anything that needs a full workspace (Compendium, prompt editor, Final Cut, Regex) is a Layer. Visited panels stay mounted and hidden so drafts survive, exactly as today. The existing pop-out button on every panel header stays.

1. The panel switcher in the header

Decision. The header carries five pinned toggles (Control Booth, Cast, Lorebooks, Story Director, Trackers), a grid button "All panels", and a find box. Clicking the active toggle closes the panel, Esc closes it too, and clicking another swaps it. The grid button opens a searchable list of every program grouped by wing, each row tagged Side, Wide or Layer, with a star to pin or unpin it. Pins are a per-person preference. Features with a panel (Story Director, trackers, TrackHare) appear in the list only while that Prop is on, and Stage tabs (Pictures, Music, Spotify, Wardrobe) only while the scene shows them, as today. The Director's Cut pill and the Update badge keep their place, left of the icons. Replaces: the 16+ round rail buttons that today each open a program.

The right column. Panels live in the column Discord uses for the member list, over the window's themed gradient with a lightly translucent surface, and are switched by header icon buttons like members, threads and pins. The Cast panel is drawn as that member list: role-style headers with counts, avatar with status dot, colored names, badge pills and a status line.

Why not an icon rail. Discord's left rail is for places, not tools. Putting tools in the header (members, threads, pins, search) is its own pattern, so panels live there. Today's left rail goes back to spaces: characters, scenes and app spaces. The sidebar's Active features rows are a second, quieter way into the same panels, as Discord shows voice activity under a channel. Control Booth stays the hub (first pinned icon), not dissolved into chips.

Keyboard and focus. The grid popout is a menu: arrow keys move, Enter opens, Esc closes and returns focus to the grid button. Opening a panel moves focus to its first control and Esc closes it. Under a Layer the header and panels are inert, as dialogs are today.

2. Cast: a members list, a profile card and an inspector

Decision. The Participants tab becomes Discord's member list: groups On Stage, NPCs, Registered, Retired with counts, avatars, a presence dot (Present or Absent) and a Main badge. Row actions that used to be inline buttons (Ping, Set Main, Present/Absent, Color, Uses Card Gradient, Delete) move to a profile card popout on avatar click and a hover bar for the two quick ones. Editing a character or your persona is a Wide inspector, since it is a long form. The reconcile card becomes a banner at the top of the list. The "You now play" swap line becomes a system message in the chat, and Casting Call becomes a popout from the user panel at the bottom of the sidebar and from the persona row.

The Compendium is a Layer (section 6), not squeezed in here.

5. Trackers: a pinned-messages style list
