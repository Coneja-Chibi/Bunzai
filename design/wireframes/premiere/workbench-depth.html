WIREFRAME. Ways to give the empty Workbench more shadow and depth. Layout, copy and tiles are today's; only light, shadow and surface change. Colours follow the accent picker (kit).

A. Ambient depth

Soft light from above. The card gets a raised edge (light top highlight), a tight contact shadow plus a wide falloff, and a pool of shadow under it. The Recent rail sits on a ledge recessed into the floor. The dock floats on frosted glass. Quietest of the three; closest to today.

B. Stacked print

Keeps the house hard offset shadow and stacks it: the card is three sheets (accent, select, black), titles carry a printed double shadow, tiles each throw a hard offset in their deck colour. A halftone dot field fades out behind the card. Graphic, poster-like, no blur.

C. Kage-e (shadow theatre)

Same materials as today: the plum card, the gold edge, the pastel tiles, the buttons. Only the light changes: a low lamp glows on the back wall, and every piece throws a soft silhouette onto it, larger and further back than a normal drop shadow, the way a puppet shadow falls on the screen. Two lamps, so the card has a strong shadow and a faint second one. All static.

D. Shadow print (B + C)

B's halftone dots on the wall, now lit by C's lamp, and B's printed stamps on the title, words and buttons. The card, tiles and dock keep C's soft puppet shadows on the wall. Same materials as today.
