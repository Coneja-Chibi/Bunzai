The shelf shows up to 14 pieces, newest first, and leaves out anything already open in a tab. It folds away on its own while you edit and returns when the bench clears. Right-click a card for its menu.

6Nodes keeps its canvas and keeps this shelf, since that is how more pieces come onto it. Only the header above changes.
  1. Header. Today the room opens on a floating box with a monospace status line and a loose Reload Studio button. Now a Playbill header carries the room name, a one-line lede, the Desk and Nodes switch, focus mode and Reload studio together. The status line becomes a quiet pill beside the tab strip.
  2. The hero. The empty state is a staged panel with light reading through it instead of a small card on black. It keeps every real state: "Your workbench is empty", "Restoring your workbench..." (call to action hidden, as today) and "We couldn't restore the workbench" (shown with the failure text). Use the wireframe switches at the top to see each.
  3. Entry points. Open Library, New pack folder and New lorebook become three cards with a deck-colored edge and a one-line promise each. Open Library is the rose primary. Same three actions, same behavior, including the saved-failure message on create.
  4. What opens here. New and purely informational: the eight kinds the bench can edit, in their deck colors, so a new user learns what the room is for. Nothing in it is interactive.
  5. Recents. The "Recent, bring one up" rail moves from a cramped strip under the dock to a shelf of portrait cards with presence (portrait or initial, name, kind, count). The hide and show control and its remembered state are kept. Rank, the 14 card cap and the context menu are unchanged.
  6. Nodes. Nodes view is a canvas, so it keeps its own layout. It inherits the new header and keeps the recents shelf.
