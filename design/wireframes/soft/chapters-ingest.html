The HarborThe Empty Nets

The nets came up empty for the third night running, and Old Tam had stopped pretending it was the weather. He stood at the rail counting the gaps between waves like a man counting his own pulse, and when Voss came up the ladder without a lantern he did not turn around.

"Sit, if you like," he said. "The storm won't care either way." He said it to the water, not to her, and it was a while before either of them spoke again. When he finally did, his voice had gone flat in a way she hadn't heard from him before. Cut clean, he said. Every line, like scissors. Not the work of anything that swam.
